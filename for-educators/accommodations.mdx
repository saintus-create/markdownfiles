# Classroom Accommodations

Accommodations level the playing field — they do not give students an unfair advantage, they remove barriers created by the disability.

## For inattentive symptoms

| Accommodation | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Extended time on tests | Compensates for slower processing and re-reading |
| Frequent check-ins during work | Prevents long periods off-task |
| Written instructions provided | Reduces reliance on auditory working memory |
| Chunked assignments | Prevents overwhelm from large tasks |
| Study guides and outlines | Reduces note-taking load; focuses attention |
| Quiet testing environment | Reduces external distractions |
| Preferential seating | Minimizes environmental distractions |

## For hyperactive-impulsive symptoms

| Accommodation | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Scheduled movement breaks | Provides legitimate outlet for physical energy |
| Flexible seating options | Allows for appropriate movement (stand-up desk, wobble seat) |
| Opportunity to pass during discussions | Reduces blurting and impulsive responses |
| Frequent feedback | Maintains on-task behavior with immediate reinforcement |
| Shortened sitting requirements | Alternate tasks requiring movement |

## For combined presentation

Students with combined presentation benefit from both sets of accommodations above, individualized to their specific functional challenges.

## Accommodations for specific assignments

### Reading assignments
- Pre-teach vocabulary and background knowledge
- Provide outlines or chapter summaries
- Allow use of audiobooks or text-to-speech tools
- Highlight key passages in advance

### Written assignments
- Provide graphic organizers and writing frames
- Accept outlines or dictated drafts as interim steps
- Offer extended time and interim check-ins
- Allow oral response as an alternative where appropriate

### Math assignments
- Allow use of calculator for computation when math reasoning is the target skill
- Reduce the number of practice problems
- Provide graph paper for alignment
- Allow work to be shown in steps with visual breaks

### Homework
- Reduce homework quantity while maintaining learning objectives
- Provide a homework planner or log
- Communicate regularly with parents about missing assignments
- Allow late submission without full penalty when disability is the factor
