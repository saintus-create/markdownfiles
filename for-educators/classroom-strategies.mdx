# Classroom Strategies

## Instructional strategies

### Delivering instructions
- Give one instruction at a time; break multi-step directions into parts
- Use visual cues alongside verbal instructions
- Check for understanding before students begin work
- Allow students to repeat instructions back in their own words

### During lessons
- Use varied instructional formats to maintain engagement (discussion, hands-on, visual)
- Keep lessons brisk and well-paced
- Provide frequent, brief breaks during long tasks
- Use movement when possible (stand to answer, pass materials, stretch breaks)

### Assignment structure
- Provide clear, written expectations for all assignments
- Break long assignments into shorter checkpoints with deadlines
- Use graphic organizers and templates to scaffold written work
- Reduce the quantity of practice items when mastery has been demonstrated

## Behavior management

### Positive behavior interventions
- Praise specific, positive behavior immediately and frequently ("I noticed you stayed in your seat for the whole lesson — great work")
- Use a reward system for target behaviors
- Establish clear, consistent rules and consequences
- Provide more positive feedback than corrective feedback

### How rewards and consequences work
- Rewards and consequences must be immediate and frequent for students with ADHD
- Delayed consequences (detention tomorrow) are less effective than immediate ones
- Varied rewards maintain motivation; rotate the reward menu regularly
- Token economy systems can bridge the gap between behavior and reward

### Structure and guidance
- Predictable routines reduce anxiety and impulsive behavior
- Provide transition warnings before switching activities
- Use visual schedules and timers to make time concrete
- Seat students away from distractions (doors, windows, high-traffic areas)

## Attention strategies

Strategies for students with distractibility and inattention:

- **Preferential seating** — near the teacher, away from distractions
- **Frequent check-ins** — brief, private prompts ("Are you on step 2?")
- **Fidget tools** — evidence suggests some students focus better with appropriate movement
- **Chunking work** — visible progress markers reduce overwhelm
- **Interest connection** — link content to student interests when possible
