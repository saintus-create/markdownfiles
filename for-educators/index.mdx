# For Educators

Teachers and educators are in a unique position to help students with ADHD achieve their full academic potential. CHADD provides free, evidence-based resources to help educators recognize ADHD, implement effective strategies, and understand students' legal rights.

## Why educator involvement matters

Students with ADHD spend most of their waking hours in school. Educator recognition, understanding, and support can make a profound difference in:

- Academic achievement and school completion
- Social development and peer relationships
- Self-esteem and long-term outcomes
- Early identification and referral for evaluation

## Recognizing ADHD in the classroom

Symptoms that may be visible in the classroom:

| Inattentive symptoms | Hyperactive-impulsive symptoms |
|---|---|
| Frequently off-task | Difficulty remaining seated |
| Appears not to listen | Talks excessively |
| Makes careless errors | Blurts out answers |
| Loses materials | Has trouble waiting turns |
| Avoids sustained tasks | Interrupts others |
| Easily distracted | Constantly "on the go" |

ADHD symptoms must be present across multiple settings and significantly impair functioning. Teachers play a critical role in completing behavior rating scales used in ADHD evaluations.
