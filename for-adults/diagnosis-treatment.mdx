# Diagnosis & Treatment in Adults

## Getting diagnosed as an adult

Many adults with ADHD were never diagnosed as children. Diagnosis in adulthood is becoming more common as awareness grows. A comprehensive adult evaluation includes:

- Clinical interview covering current symptoms and childhood history
- Rating scales and self-report questionnaires
- Review of school records when available
- Medical history to rule out other conditions
- Collateral information from a partner, family member, or close friend

## Treatment for adults

Treatment typically combines medication, behavioral strategies, and psychosocial support.

### Medication

The same medications used for children are generally effective for adults, though dosing and formulation preferences may differ:

- **Stimulants** (first-line): methylphenidate, amphetamine-based medications
- **Non-stimulants**: atomoxetine, viloxazine, bupropion (off-label)
- Many adults require medication throughout the day, including evenings

### Medication management tips

- Take medication as prescribed — do not adjust doses independently
- Inform your prescriber of all other medications, supplements, and substances
- Know the laws for traveling with controlled substances
- Report side effects promptly rather than stopping medication without guidance

### Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT)

CBT adapted for ADHD is highly effective for adults, targeting:

- Organization and time management
- Reducing procrastination
- Managing emotional responses
- Building coping strategies for persistent challenges

### ADHD coaching

ADHD coaching focuses on goal-setting, accountability, and practical strategies for managing daily life. Coaches are not therapists but can be valuable complements to clinical treatment.

## Insurance and access

- Request a letter of medical necessity from your provider for insurance appeals
- Federally Qualified Health Centers offer sliding-scale fees
- Telehealth has significantly expanded access to ADHD evaluation and treatment
