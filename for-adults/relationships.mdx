# Relationships & Social Skills

ADHD can significantly affect romantic partnerships, friendships, and family relationships. Understanding these dynamics is the first step toward building healthier connections.

## How ADHD affects relationships

Common ADHD-related relationship challenges include:

- **Inattention** — appearing distracted or uninterested during conversations
- **Impulsivity** — interrupting, speaking without thinking, making hasty decisions
- **Forgetfulness** — missing anniversaries, appointments, or commitments
- **Emotional dysregulation** — difficulty managing frustration or anger
- **Hyperfocus** — intense attention early in a relationship followed by withdrawal
- **Disorganization** — creating household friction around chores and finances

## Strategies for stronger relationships

### Communication
- Practice active listening: put away distractions, make eye contact, summarize what you heard
- Use structured conversation tools (e.g., scheduled weekly check-ins)
- Write down commitments immediately after making them

### For couples
- Education for both partners helps reduce misattribution ("they don't care" vs. "ADHD affects memory")
- Couples therapy with a therapist experienced in ADHD is highly effective
- Divide household responsibilities based on strengths rather than convention

### Managing money together
- Automate bill payments to prevent missed payments
- Set shared financial goals and review them together regularly
- Use separate "fun money" allowances to reduce conflict over impulse purchases

## ADHD and driving

Adults with ADHD have higher rates of motor vehicle accidents. Strategies include:
- Avoid distractions (phone, complex conversations while driving)
- Do not drive while overly fatigued
- Consider whether medication timing aligns with driving hours
