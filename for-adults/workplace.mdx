# Workplace Issues

Adults with ADHD often struggle in the workplace despite strong intelligence and capability. Common challenges include inconsistent performance, missed deadlines, disorganization, and difficulty managing complex projects.

## Strategies for success at work

### Organization
- Use a single task management system (digital or paper — choose one and stick with it)
- Break large projects into smaller, timed tasks
- Use calendar reminders for all deadlines and meetings
- Keep a clear, uncluttered workspace

### Time management
- Use time-blocking to schedule focused work periods
- Set visual timers to stay on task
- Build buffer time between tasks and appointments
- Schedule the most demanding work during your peak attention hours

### Communication
- Confirm verbal instructions in writing (follow up meetings with a brief email summary)
- Ask for deadlines in writing
- Communicate proactively when a deadline is at risk

## Legal protections

Adults with ADHD may qualify for workplace accommodations under two federal laws:

### Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)

- Applies to employers with 15 or more employees
- Prohibits discrimination based on disability
- Requires employers to provide **reasonable accommodations** unless doing so creates undue hardship
- Examples: flexible scheduling, written instructions, reduced distractions, extended deadlines

### Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Section 504)

- Applies to employers receiving federal funding
- Similar protections as ADA

## Requesting accommodations

You are not required to disclose your ADHD diagnosis — only that you have a medical condition requiring accommodation. Work with your healthcare provider to document the functional limitations your ADHD creates in the workplace.

## Job discrimination

If you believe you have experienced discrimination due to ADHD, you can file a complaint with the **Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)** within 180 days of the incident.
