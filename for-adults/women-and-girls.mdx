# Women & Girls with ADHD

ADHD in women and girls is frequently **underdiagnosed and undertreated**. This is partly because ADHD in females often presents differently than the hyperactive-impulsive profile most commonly recognized in boys.

## How ADHD presents in women and girls

- **Predominantly inattentive presentation** is more common in girls
- Internalized symptoms: daydreaming, disorganization, emotional sensitivity
- Greater tendency to mask or camouflage symptoms through extra effort
- Higher rates of anxiety and depression as coexisting conditions
- Social difficulties may be more prominent than behavioral problems

## Why women are underdiagnosed

- Diagnostic criteria were historically developed based on research on boys
- Girls with inattentive ADHD are less disruptive and less likely to be referred for evaluation
- Girls often develop compensatory strategies that mask symptoms in childhood
- Symptoms may become more apparent during hormonal transitions (puberty, menstruation, pregnancy, perimenopause)

## Hormonal influences

Estrogen plays a role in regulating dopamine function. As a result, many women with ADHD experience fluctuating symptom severity tied to their menstrual cycle, with symptoms often worsening in the week before menstruation.

### Pregnancy and medication
- Discuss medication decisions with your prescriber before becoming pregnant
- Some women choose to continue medication; others pause
- Non-pharmacological support (coaching, therapy, organizational systems) becomes more important during pregnancy

## Lifestyle supports

- **Sleep** is particularly important — establish a consistent routine
- **Exercise** helps regulate dopamine and reduces ADHD symptoms
- **Stress reduction** — ADHD symptoms worsen under high stress
- **Community** — peer support groups for women with ADHD reduce isolation
