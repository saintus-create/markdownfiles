# For Teens & Children

Children and teens who have ADHD often display similar symptoms, including inattention, impulsivity, and hyperactivity. Some symptoms you experienced in childhood may change as you get older. You may have been more hyperactive as a child but less so as a teen. As you get older, you might struggle more with **executive function skills**, like completing homework, handing in assignments, or focusing on what the teacher says during class.

## Understanding your symptoms

The best way to manage ADHD symptoms is to understand how they affect **you specifically**. Some children and teens have trouble with time management, while others might struggle with blurting out in class or managing their emotions.

### Common challenges

- Forgetting homework or losing track of assignments
- Difficulty staying focused during class
- Impulsively saying things without thinking first
- Feeling restless or having trouble sitting still
- Running late or losing track of time
- Managing emotions, especially frustration

## Your strengths

You could find that some of your biggest strengths come from having ADHD:

- **Creative thinking** — seeing problems and solutions others miss
- **Hyper-focus** — the ability to intensely concentrate on subjects that interest you
- **Quick processing** — understanding concepts rapidly in areas of interest
- **Adventurousness** — willingness to try new things and take on challenges
- **Energy** — high drive and enthusiasm when engaged

## Getting help

If you think you might have ADHD, talk with your parents or caregivers about finding a healthcare professional who specializes in diagnosing and treating ADHD in children and teens.
