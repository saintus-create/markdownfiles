# Treatment Strategies

## First-line recommendations

### Children (age 6+)
Clinical practice guidelines (AAP, 2019) recommend:
- **FDA-approved medications** as first-line treatment
- **Behavior therapy** as a co-treatment
- Parent Training in Behavior Management (PTBM) as essential

### Preschool children (age 4–5)
- **Behavior therapy first** — PTBM before medication
- Methylphenidate if behavior therapy is insufficient and symptoms are severe

### Adults
- **Stimulant medications** are first-line
- **CBT adapted for ADHD** is the most evidence-supported psychosocial treatment
- Combined treatment is generally more effective than either alone

## Medications

### Stimulants
- Methylphenidate: Ritalin, Concerta, Metadate, Daytrana
- Amphetamine-based: Adderall, Vyvanse, Dexedrine, Evekeo
- Response rates: 70–80%; if one stimulant is ineffective, try the other class

### Non-stimulants
- **Atomoxetine (Strattera)**: SNRI; FDA-approved; full effect in 4–6 weeks
- **Viloxazine (Qelbree)**: Newer SNRI; FDA-approved for children and adolescents
- **Guanfacine ER (Intuniv)** / **Clonidine ER (Kapvay)**: Alpha-2 agonists; approved for children
- **Bupropion**: Off-label; useful when depression is comorbid

### Monitoring
- Monitor height and weight at each visit
- Review cardiovascular risk at baseline; EKG if history indicates
- Assess for emergence of psychiatric symptoms
- Evaluate sleep, appetite, and mood at each follow-up

## Psychosocial treatments

### Evidence-based options
| Treatment | Best evidence for |
|---|---|
| Parent Training in Behavior Management | Children under 12 |
| Behavioral classroom interventions | School-age children |
| Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT) | Adolescents and adults |
| Social skills training | Children with significant social impairment |
| Organization skills training | Older children and adolescents |

### CBT for adult ADHD
Effective CBT modules for adults include:
- Organization and planning skills
- Reducing distractibility
- Adaptive thinking (addressing negative thought patterns)
- Managing procrastination
- Emotion regulation

## Treatment of coexisting conditions

Treat ADHD and coexisting conditions simultaneously when possible. Consider:
- If anxiety is present: does it predate ADHD onset, or is it secondary to chronic ADHD-related failure?
- Depression may respond to stimulant treatment alone in some cases
- Sleep problems often improve with ADHD treatment but may require independent intervention
