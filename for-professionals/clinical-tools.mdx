# Clinical Tools

## Rating scales

### For children (parent/teacher report)
| Scale | Ages | Domains |
|---|---|---|
| Conners 3 | 6–18 | ADHD symptoms, inattention, hyperactivity, executive function |
| BASC-3 | 2–21 | Broad behavioral and emotional functioning |
| BRIEF-2 | 5–18 | Executive function |
| Vanderbilt ADHD Scales | 6–12 | ADHD symptoms and coexisting conditions (free) |
| SNAP-IV | 6–18 | ADHD symptoms, ODD (free) |

### For adults (self-report)
| Scale | Notes |
|---|---|
| ASRS-v1.1 (Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale) | WHO-developed; 6-item screener; free |
| CAARS | Comprehensive; normed for adults |
| BAARS-IV | Brown ADD Rating Scales; executive function focus |

## Clinical practice guidelines

### American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) — 2019
- Expands diagnosis to ages 4–18
- Recommends behavior therapy as first-line for preschoolers
- Combined treatment for school-age children
- Recognizes ADHD as chronic condition requiring ongoing management

### American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry (AACAP) — Practice Parameter
- Comprehensive evaluation standards
- Medication monitoring protocols
- Guidance on coexisting conditions

### Adult ADHD guidelines
- Canadian ADHD Resource Alliance (CADDRA) guidelines are widely referenced
- UK NICE guidelines provide international perspective

## Quick reference: prescribing pearls

- Start low, titrate slowly — individual response varies significantly
- Evaluate effectiveness across settings (school/work, not just at home)
- Once-daily formulations improve adherence
- Rebound effect (late-day irritability) may indicate need for longer-acting or afternoon booster dose
- Medication holidays are rarely necessary and may disrupt functioning
