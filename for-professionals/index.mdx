# For Professionals

An estimated **15 million individuals in America have ADHD**. Without identification and proper treatment, ADHD may have serious consequences including school failure, family stress, depression, relationship problems, substance abuse, delinquency, and job failure.

With identification, proper treatment, and management, individuals with ADHD can lead successful lives and even thrive. One of the key components to thriving is the support of a qualified professional team.

## Professional roles in ADHD care

Professionals who work with people affected by ADHD include:

| Profession | Primary role |
|---|---|
| Psychiatrists | Diagnosis, medication management |
| Pediatricians / Family physicians | First-line diagnosis in children; medication |
| Psychologists | Comprehensive evaluation; psychotherapy; testing |
| Neurologists | When neurological factors are present |
| Social workers | Psychosocial support; case management |
| Nurse practitioners | Evaluation and medication in many states |
| Therapists / Counselors | CBT, behavior therapy, family therapy |
| Teachers | Behavioral interventions; referral |
| ADHD Coaches | Non-clinical support; goal-setting; accountability |

## What is ADHD?

ADHD is a neurodevelopmental disorder described as a persistent or ongoing pattern of **inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity** that gets in the way of daily life or typical development. Individuals may also have difficulties with:

- Maintaining attention over time
- Executive function (initiating, organizing, managing tasks)
- Working memory

The DSM-5 (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, published by the American Psychiatric Association) provides the diagnostic criteria used in clinical practice.
