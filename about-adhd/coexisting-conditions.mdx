# Coexisting Conditions

ADHD rarely occurs in isolation. Research consistently shows that **two-thirds or more of individuals with ADHD** have at least one other coexisting condition. Identifying and treating these conditions is essential for effective management.

## Common coexisting conditions

### Disruptive behavior disorders

- **Oppositional Defiant Disorder (ODD)** — up to 50% of children with ADHD
- **Conduct Disorder (CD)** — up to 25% of children with ADHD
- These disorders involve patterns of defiant, hostile, or antisocial behavior

### Mood and anxiety disorders

- **Depression** — affects a significant portion of people with ADHD across all ages
- **Anxiety disorders** — among the most common coexisting conditions in adults
- Untreated ADHD can contribute to secondary depression from chronic failure and frustration

### Sleep disorders

- Children and adults with ADHD frequently experience difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking rested
- Sleep problems can worsen ADHD symptoms, creating a difficult cycle
- Both behavioral interventions and medication adjustments may help

### Tic disorders and Tourette syndrome

- Approximately 20% of children with ADHD have tics
- Stimulant medications do not cause tics but may temporarily worsen them in some individuals
- Management requires coordination between treatments

### Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)

- ADHD and ASD frequently co-occur
- The DSM-5 (updated from DSM-IV) now permits dual diagnosis
- Combined presentations require individualized treatment planning

### Learning disabilities

- **Dyslexia** (reading disorder) is common
- **Dyscalculia** (math disorder)
- **Dysgraphia** (writing disorder)
- These are separate from ADHD but often present together

## Why coexisting conditions matter

Unrecognized coexisting conditions are a primary reason why treatment for ADHD alone may not produce expected results. A comprehensive evaluation should always screen for common coexisting conditions.
