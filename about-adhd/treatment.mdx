# Treatment

ADHD is a highly treatable condition. The most effective approach combines multiple strategies tailored to the individual's age, symptom severity, and life circumstances.

## Evidence-based treatments

### Medication

Medication is the most studied and effective single treatment for ADHD. Two main categories:

**Stimulants**
- Most commonly prescribed; effective for approximately 70–80% of people with ADHD
- Examples: methylphenidate (Ritalin, Concerta), amphetamine salts (Adderall, Vyvanse)
- Work by increasing dopamine and norepinephrine availability in the brain

**Non-stimulants**
- Used when stimulants are ineffective or cause intolerable side effects
- Examples: atomoxetine (Strattera), viloxazine (Qelbree), guanfacine (Intuniv)
- Generally take longer to show effect than stimulants

### Behavioral therapy

- **Behavior management training** — teaches parents and caregivers specific strategies
- **Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT)** — especially effective for adults
- **Social skills training** — helps with peer relationships and communication
- **Parent training and education** — essential for children, particularly under age 12

### Combined treatment

Research consistently shows that **combined treatment** (medication + behavioral intervention) produces better outcomes than either approach alone, particularly for:
- Improving academic performance
- Reducing family stress
- Building social skills
- Addressing coexisting conditions

## Complementary approaches

While not replacements for evidence-based treatment, some individuals benefit from:

| Approach | Evidence level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise | Moderate | Regular aerobic exercise can reduce ADHD symptoms |
| ADHD coaching | Moderate | Helps with goal-setting, organization, time management |
| Dietary adjustments | Limited | Some benefit from reducing artificial dyes; omega-3s may help mildly |
| Mindfulness/meditation | Limited | May improve attention and reduce stress |
| Neurofeedback | Inconsistent | Not yet recommended as first-line treatment |

## Finding low-cost treatment

- Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs) offer sliding-scale fees
- State Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) for uninsured children
- Pharmaceutical assistance programs through manufacturers
- University training clinics often offer lower-cost evaluations
