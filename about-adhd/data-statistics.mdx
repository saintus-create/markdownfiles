# Data & Statistics

## Prevalence

| Population | Estimated prevalence |
|---|---|
| School-age children (U.S.) | 11% |
| Adults (U.S.) | ~15.5 million diagnosed |
| Global (children) | ~5–7% |
| Persistence into adulthood | More than 75% of childhood cases |

## Diagnosis trends

- ADHD diagnosis rates have increased over the past two decades, likely due to better awareness and improved diagnostic practices
- Boys are diagnosed approximately twice as often as girls, though the actual gender difference in prevalence is smaller — girls are frequently underdiagnosed
- Diagnosis in adults has grown as awareness of adult ADHD has increased

## Outcomes without treatment

Without identification and proper treatment, ADHD is associated with:

- School failure and dropout
- Family stress and disruption
- Depression and other mental health conditions
- Problems with relationships
- Substance abuse
- Delinquency and involvement with the criminal justice system
- Accidental injuries (particularly motor vehicle accidents)
- Employment instability and job failure

## Outcomes with treatment

With appropriate identification, treatment, and management, individuals with ADHD can lead successful and fulfilling lives. Early intervention is strongly associated with better long-term outcomes in:

- Academic achievement
- Employment and career success
- Relationship stability
- Overall quality of life

## Economic impact

ADHD has a significant societal cost, including:

- Higher rates of emergency room visits and hospitalizations
- Lost workplace productivity
- Special education and support services
- Criminal justice system involvement

## ADHD and long-term outcomes

Research shows that adults with untreated ADHD are more likely to:

- Change jobs frequently
- Experience financial difficulties
- Have higher rates of motor vehicle accidents
- Report lower life satisfaction

Treatment significantly reduces these risks.
