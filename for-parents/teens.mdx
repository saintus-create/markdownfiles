# Teens & Young Adults with ADHD

Adolescence brings new challenges for teens with ADHD — greater academic demands, increased social complexity, driving, and the beginning of independence. Parenting a teen with ADHD requires adapting your approach as your child grows.

## How ADHD changes in adolescence

- Hyperactivity often decreases or becomes internalized as restlessness
- Executive function demands increase significantly (planning, prioritizing, long-term projects)
- Emotional dysregulation can become more pronounced
- Peer influence becomes more powerful
- Risk-taking behaviors may increase

## Parenting strategies for teens

- **Shift toward collaboration**: involve your teen in setting rules and consequences
- **Focus on autonomy**: build independence gradually with scaffolded support
- **Stay connected**: maintain open, non-judgmental communication
- **Separate the disorder from the person**: address behavior without attacking character
- **Build on strengths**: identify and nurture your teen's talents and interests

## Driving and ADHD

Teens with ADHD have higher rates of motor vehicle accidents. Before your teen drives:

- Ensure ADHD is well-managed with treatment
- Consider delaying licensure until symptoms are well-controlled
- Establish strict rules about phone use, passenger limits, and curfews
- Increase supervised driving hours beyond minimum requirements

## Medication diversion

Stimulant medications are controlled substances that may be sought by peers. Teach your teen:
- Never share medication — it is illegal and dangerous
- Keep medication secure
- What to say if peers ask for medication

## College and ADHD

The transition to college removes many of the external structures that helped in high school. Prepare by:

- Registering with the college disability services office before classes begin
- Developing strong organizational systems before leaving home
- Researching colleges with strong ADHD support programs
- Planning medication management independently (no parent to remind them)

Disclosing ADHD during college admissions is generally not recommended on applications, but disclosing to the disability services office after enrollment is important for accessing accommodations.
