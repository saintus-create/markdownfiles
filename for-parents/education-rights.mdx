# Education Rights for Students with ADHD

Students with ADHD are protected under two major federal laws. Understanding these protections helps parents advocate effectively for their child.

## Two federal frameworks

| | Section 504 | IDEA |
|---|---|---|
| **Full name** | Rehabilitation Act of 1973, Section 504 | Individuals with Disabilities Education Act |
| **Purpose** | Prevents discrimination; ensures equal access | Ensures a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) |
| **Eligibility** | Broader — any disability that limits a major life activity | Narrower — 13 specific disability categories |
| **Plan type** | 504 Plan | Individualized Education Program (IEP) |
| **Applies to** | All schools receiving federal funding | Public schools only |
| **Services** | Accommodations | Accommodations + specialized instruction and services |

## 504 Plans

A 504 Plan documents accommodations a school must provide. Common accommodations for students with ADHD include:

- Extended time on tests and assignments
- Preferential seating (away from distractions)
- Breaks during long tasks
- Instructions repeated or provided in writing
- Reduced homework load or modified assignments
- Organizational tools and supports
- Access to a quiet testing environment

## Individualized Education Programs (IEPs)

An IEP is appropriate when a student needs **specialized instruction** in addition to accommodations. An IEP includes:

- Current levels of academic performance
- Measurable annual goals
- Specific services to be provided
- How progress will be measured
- Transition planning (for students age 16+)

## Requesting an evaluation

1. Submit a written request to the school principal or special education director
2. Request must be responded to within a set timeframe (varies by state, typically 60 days)
3. The school must provide evaluation at no cost to parents
4. Parents must provide written consent before evaluation begins

## Appealing decisions

If you disagree with an eligibility determination or the content of an IEP or 504 Plan:
- Request a meeting with the school team to discuss concerns
- File a state complaint with your state education agency
- Request mediation (free and voluntary)
- Request a due process hearing (formal legal process)

## Tips for working with schools

- Put requests and agreements in writing
- Keep records of all correspondence
- Attend all meetings and bring a support person if needed
- Learn your state's specific timelines and procedures
- Connect with your state's Parent Training and Information (PTI) center for free advocacy help
