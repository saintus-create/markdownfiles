# For Parents & Caregivers

While ADHD is believed to be hereditary, effectively managing your child's symptoms can affect both the severity of the disorder and the development of more serious problems over time. **Early intervention holds the key to positive outcomes** for your child. The earlier you address your child's ADHD symptoms, the better the chances of improving behavior, preventing school failure, and building healthy relationships.

## Your role as a parent

As a parent of a child with ADHD, you are not alone. CHADD estimates that millions of parents are raising children with ADHD. Your role is critical:

- Advocate for appropriate evaluation and treatment
- Collaborate with schools to establish needed supports
- Build consistent structure and routines at home
- Model and teach coping skills
- Celebrate your child's strengths alongside addressing challenges

## What to expect

ADHD requires ongoing management, not a one-time fix. Treatment effectiveness should be reviewed regularly as your child grows, and strategies may need adjustment during key transitions (starting school, entering adolescence, transitioning to college or work).
